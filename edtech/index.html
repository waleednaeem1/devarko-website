Why us

The hard parts of e-learning are never the course list

Anyone can build a page that lists courses. The parts that decide whether a learning platform survives contact with real users are video that plays on a weak connection, payments that cope with instalments and coupons, access rules that stop a lapsed student watching paid content, and an admin panel a non-technical coordinator can run without calling you.

We have built all of that and we maintain it. Bright Future delivers private training and university-level courses across Kuwait on web, iOS and Android. Ilmversity runs course delivery, assessments and administration for schools nationwide. VetandTech puts RACE-approved CE webinars in front of veterinary professionals in the USA.

Questions

What clients ask us first.

What makes an EdTech build different from a normal web app?
Three things usually: video that has to stream securely and cheaply at scale, access rules that decide who can see what and for how long, and payment logic that is rarely a simple one-off charge. Get those wrong and the product works fine in a demo then falls apart in a term. We have already solved them in production, which is most of what you are buying.
Can you build the mobile apps as well as the platform?
Yes, and we would argue you should do both together. The Bright Future student apps for iOS and Android run on the same Laravel API as the web platform, so a change to an enrolment rule does not have to be written three times. Building the apps later, against an API that was never designed for them, is how teams end up with three products that disagree.
Do you work with schools, or universities, or training companies?
All three, and they want different things. A school LMS is about timetables, sections, attendance and parents. A university or training provider is about paid enrolment, cohorts and certification. A professional body is about credits and compliance. We have shipped for each, so we can tell you early which parts of your brief are normal and which are unusual.
We already have an LMS. Can you improve it rather than replace it?
Often, yes, and it is usually the cheaper answer. Most complaints we hear about an existing LMS are about video delivery, payments or reporting rather than the whole system. We will read what you have before recommending a rebuild, and if replacing one module fixes the problem we will say so.

Building something for learners?

Tell us who is learning, how they pay, and what has to work on a phone with two bars of signal. We will tell you which parts are straightforward and which ones tend to bite.
