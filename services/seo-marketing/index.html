Overview

SEO that starts in the codebase

Most of the SEO problems we get asked to look at are engineering problems wearing a marketing hat. Pages that render entirely in JavaScript and never get indexed properly. A sitemap nobody has regenerated since launch. Product and course pages with no structured data at all. Hero images shipped at four times the size they are displayed at. We find those in an audit and then fix them in the repository, because we are the people who write the code anyway.

The rest of it is slower work: deciding which searches actually matter to your business, writing pages that answer them properly, and giving search engines enough time to notice. We will show you what moved and what did not each month. We will not pretend a three-month-old site can outrank an established competitor on its biggest keyword, and we would rather tell you that at the start than bill you for six months of finding out.

What's included

What you get with SEO & Digital Marketing.

01

Technical SEO audits

We crawl the site the way a search engine does and check what genuinely gets indexed: render-blocking JavaScript, redirect chains, canonical conflicts, orphan pages, a robots.txt quietly blocking half the catalogue. You get a prioritised list with an effort estimate beside each item, not a sixty-page PDF.

02

Structured data and rich results

JSON-LD that describes what your pages actually are: courses, organisations, articles, FAQs, breadcrumbs, products, local business. We validate every type in Search Console and clear the warnings, rather than shipping markup and hoping it takes.

03

Performance and Core Web Vitals

LCP, CLS and INP are engineering problems, so we fix them at source: image formats and sizing, font loading, bundle splitting, cache headers, CDN configuration. On media-heavy sites we serve video through Bunny CDN so a hero video does not sink the page score.

04

Site architecture and on-page

URL structure, internal linking, heading hierarchy, and titles and descriptions handled per template where that makes more sense than per page. If you sell across countries or languages, we set up hreflang and localised routes properly: our work already spans Kuwait, Pakistan and the USA.

05

Content that earns rankings

We map the searches that lead to real enquiries, then brief and structure the pages that answer them. Your people know the domain better than any writer we could hire, so we usually interview them and do the drafting and editing around that.

06

Analytics, tracking and reporting

GA4 and Search Console set up under your own accounts, conversion and event tracking that matches how your business defines a lead, and a monthly report written in plain English. If you run Google or Meta campaigns, we make sure the landing pages and tracking hold up their end.

How we work

From first call to live.

01

Audit and baseline

We crawl the site, pull whatever Search Console history exists, measure Core Web Vitals on real pages and write down exactly where you stand today. Everything that follows gets compared against that baseline.

02

Fix the technical debt

The findings go into the repository as ordinary development work: reviewed, checked by our QA testers and released the way any other change ships. Most of the quick wins go live in the first few weeks.

03

Structure and content

Keyword and intent mapping first, then information architecture, schema and a publishing plan you can realistically keep up with. Eight good pages a quarter beat forty thin ones, every time.

04

Measure and adjust

A monthly review of rankings, traffic, conversions and what changed technically. We tell you what moved, what did not, and what we would do next: including when the honest answer is to spend the budget somewhere else.

Questions

Things clients ask us.

How long before we see any results?
Technical work shows first. Indexation and crawl errors move within weeks of a release, and Core Web Vitals field data updates on a rolling 28-day window, so give it about a month before the numbers reflect a fix. Ranking and traffic movement is a three-to-six-month conversation, and longer on a new domain or in a crowded market. If your budget only stretches to three months, say so: SEO is a poor fit for a short runway, and we would rather point you at paid search than take the retainer.
Can you guarantee we'll rank first for our main keyword?
No, and nobody honest can. The ranking systems are not ours to control and they change without notice. A guarantee usually means one of three things: the agency is quietly buying ads, it has picked a phrase almost nobody searches for, or it expects to be gone before the renewal. What we do commit to is the work itself: the technical state of the site, the pages published, the schema validated, and a monthly report showing what moved and what did not.
Do we start with technical SEO or with content?
Technical, but only the parts blocking indexing and rendering: a page a crawler cannot see properly earns nothing however well it is written. Once the site is crawlable, quick enough and correctly marked up, technical work hits a ceiling fast; shaving another fraction of a second off a page that already passes rarely changes a position. After that it is content, internal linking and patience. And if the site is already sound, we will tell you, rather than sell an audit designed to find nothing.
If we invest in SEO, do we still need to run paid ads?
Usually yes, at least while organic is still building. Ads buy traffic today and, more usefully, show within weeks which searches actually convert: the best brief a content plan can have. The trade-off is how the cost behaves: ads stop the moment you stop paying, organic pages keep working but take months to arrive. Know what we are, though: we build the landing pages, tracking and product feeds campaigns depend on; we are not a media-buying agency, and for serious ongoing ad spend you want a specialist.
How do you measure whether any of it worked?
We record a baseline before touching anything: crawl results, whatever Search Console history exists, Core Web Vitals on real pages, and how many enquiries the site produces today. The monthly report then tracks a short list: pages indexed, impressions and clicks split into branded and non-branded queries, Vitals, and the conversions your business counts as a lead. Splitting branded from non-branded matters, because someone who heard your name at a conference and typed it into Google is not evidence that the SEO worked. Attribution is never clean, and where an algorithm update or a seasonal dip explains a number better than our work does, that is what the report will say.
